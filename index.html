The closed notch carries the signal: an agent waiting on an answer looks different from an agent that has finished, and both look different from a chess game you're losing. You get the news without opening anything.

Answer without leaving

Pro turns the row into Approve, Always allow and Reject, so a permission prompt costs you a click instead of a context switch. Free gets five approvals a day; after that the request falls through to the terminal exactly as it does today.

If Dyno Chess isn't running, or is quit mid-decision, the bridge does nothing at all and your agent behaves exactly as it would without it. Nothing about a session leaves the Mac: the app still ships with no network entitlement, and the hook reaches it over a local socket inside your own account. Questions.

Why isn't it on the Mac App Store?

Because it ships Stockfish, and Stockfish is licensed under the GPLv3, which is widely held to be incompatible with the App Store's terms. Downloading it here is the honest option rather than a worse one: the build is signed with a Developer ID and notarized by Apple, so Gatekeeper is satisfied, and updates come straight from this page.

Does it need an internet connection?

No, and it cannot use one. The app ships without the network entitlement at all, so this is verifiable rather than a promise — run codesign -d --entitlements :- "Dyno Chess.app" and look for yourself. There is no account, no sign-in and no analytics.

What happens when the trial ends?

Nothing breaks. You get 14 days of Pro when you first launch it, and after that the app quietly becomes the free version. The board, the island and all nine opponents keep working forever. You will never find your chess game held hostage.

How many Macs does one licence cover?

Three. The licence is a signed file checked on your Mac, so activating does not require the internet either.

Can I get a refund?

Yes, within 14 days, no reason required. Email dyno@bixxter.com and it gets refunded in full — you don't have to explain yourself. The refund policy spells out the details.

Who am I buying from, and how do I get help?

Dyno Chess is made and sold by Asatulla Dadabaev — one person, not a company. Support, refunds and lost licence keys all go to dyno@bixxter.com, and it's read by a person. The privacy policy and terms of sale are on one page.

Which engine is it, and is the source available?

Stockfish, running as a separate process over UCI, exactly as its licence intends. The full GPLv3 text ships inside the disk image alongside a link to the exact source revision the bundled binary was built from.

Three puzzles a day — is that not a bit stingy?

It's the whole feature, not a sample: you get your real rating, the rank ladder, the day streak and the theme breakdown from the first puzzle, and the app picks each one to sit just above your level. Only the count is limited. Three is about a coffee break, which is what a notch-sized board is for — and if you want to sit down and grind fifty, that's what Pro removes. The puzzles themselves come from the Lichess database, which is public domain and free forever at the source; we'd rather be upfront that what you're paying for is the volume and the machinery, not the file.

What does the Claude Code integration actually do?

Three things. It shows your live sessions in a strip under the board — the project, what the session is doing and how long it has been at it. It signals in the closed notch when one of them is blocked on you or has finished, so you don't keep tabbing back to check. And clicking a row takes you to the terminal that session is running in: Terminal.app and iTerm2 land on the exact tab, everything else gets raised to the front. On Pro you can answer the permission prompt from the notch itself; on Free you get five approvals a day and then it asks in the terminal as usual. It uses Claude Code's documented hooks and nothing else — no screen reading, no accessibility API, no polling of your files.

Can it break my agent?

It is built so it cannot. The hook exits successfully on every path it has, including a crash, a missing binary or a bad argument, and a hook that returns no decision means Claude Code carries on through its normal permission flow. If Dyno Chess isn't running, or you quit it halfway through a decision, the tool call proceeds. Hooks go into ~/.claude/settings.json only when you press Install in Settings → Agents, after a timestamped backup, appended next to your own entries rather than over them — and Remove takes out only the ones we added. If the app is deleted, the helper removes its own entries and gets out of the way.

You say it has no network access — but it can see my agent?

Both are true, and the second is why the first still holds. Claude Code runs the hook, the hook hands the event to the app over a local socket inside your own account — file permissions, a same-user check and a per-install secret — and no packet is involved, so the network entitlement is still absent and codesign still proves it. What did change in 0.3 is that the same output now lists read-write exceptions for ~/.claude and ~/.dynochess, and Apple Events for the terminals it can jump to. All of them are named in the privacy policy, and none of them can send anything anywhere.
